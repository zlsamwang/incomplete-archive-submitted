# Design Document — A Voice That Came Late

*(working title kept: "The Incomplete Archive")*

## Premise

You were eight years old on September 14, waiting at this archive for your
mother, who had promised in writing to be back by 4:30 PM and take you to
dinner before catching her intercity bus out of town. She didn't come while
you were still there. Your grandmother picked you up instead. For years,
the day compressed into one sentence: *she promised, and she didn't come.*

The archive is relocating, and former participants in its children's
recording program have been invited back to collect their personal tape
copies. You've come for yours. The game's central factual question — *did
she come, and if so, when?* — resolves within the first half of the game.
The question the game spends the rest of its runtime on is: **now that you
know what happened, what do you do with it?**

## Chronology (the fixed events)

This is the ground truth the entire evidence set was checked against.
Nothing in the game contradicts it; the player simply doesn't have it all
at once.

| Time | Event | Evidence |

| 3:30 PM | The Children's Recording Project session begins. | `AR-01` |

The evidence can establish the reported transport delay and that a
diversion existed that day. It cannot establish every minute of Elena's
journey, or prove that she could not have planned differently — those are
left open on purpose (see below).

## Structure

Four rooms — **Reception & Activity Room** (hub, combining what were
originally two separate rooms), **Photograph Gallery**, **Listening
Room**, and **Storage & Catalog Room** (also originally two rooms, folded
into one) — twenty-one evidence items, and a final "Writing the Note"
scene where the player assembles a short note to leave with their
recording. (An earlier pass used six separate rooms; folded down to four
after feedback that six felt like too many to navigate for this amount of
content — no evidence was cut, only regrouped.) There is no time pressure
and no requirement to find every item before writing — the desk is
reachable from Reception at any time, and a fact only becomes available in
the sentence bank once its supporting evidence has actually been found.

## The three discoveries

### 1. She came, after you had already gone
- **Evidence:** `RR-04` (front desk duty log: grandmother's pickup at 5:20
  PM), `SR-01` (visitor sign-in log: Elena's arrival at 5:47 PM).
- **What it establishes:** Elena did arrive at the archive that day —
  twenty-seven minutes after Jamie had already left. She was not lying,
  all those years, when she said she came.
- **What it does not establish:** anything about her state of mind, or
  whether the twenty-seven minutes should matter to how the player feels
  about it.

### 2. She tried to explain, and partly did
- **Evidence:** `SR-02` (phone slip: the 4:45 call about the Route 12
  diversion), `SR-03` (a public transit notice independently confirming
  the diversion), `AR-03` (Elena's advance note, promising 4:30 and
  acknowledging in her own words that the timing was already tight).
- **What it establishes:** a real disruption existed that afternoon, and
  Elena called ahead — before the session even ended — to ask for more
  time. Both are documented independently of anything she said later.
- **What it does not establish:** whether she could have left home earlier
  and avoided the timing problem altogether. No record touches when she
  left the house.

### 3. The message that was never delivered
- **Evidence:** `RR-03` (Jamie's personal recording, ending on the
  question "can you play it for her?"), `CO-02` (duplication record:
  personal copy sealed at 5:05, before Elena's arrival), `CO-04` (policy
  memo: master reels are always kept separate, regardless of later
  additions, and always available on request), `LR-01` (the master reel
  itself, containing Elena's message).
- **What it establishes:** Elena did leave something. It was filed
  correctly and never claimed, because nobody — including Jamie — ever
  requested it until tonight (confirmed independently by `LR-02`, the
  listening room's request log).
- **What it does not establish:** what happened between that day and now,
  or why the relationship grew distant afterward.

These three discoveries are deliberately spread across all four rooms:
Reception and Storage hold the two competing timestamps of Discovery 1;
Reception's advance note (in its activity corner) and Storage's phone slip
and transit notice build Discovery 2; and Discovery 3 requires visiting
Reception, Storage, and Listening in turn — the personal recording poses a
question, Storage's catalog corner explains the policy and timing that
kept the answer separate, and the Listening Room is the answer itself.

## Evidence matrix

**Established facts** (the record actually supports these):
- Elena dropped Jamie off on time at 3:25 PM and signed the sheet herself.
- Elena left a written note that afternoon promising to return by 4:30 PM,
  and assessed in her own words that the timing was already tight.
- The session ran its standard 3:30–5:00 PM schedule.
- Elena called the archive at 4:45 PM to say she'd been caught in a
  diversion on Route 12 and could not give a reliable arrival time.
- A real transit diversion affected Route 12 that afternoon, independent
  of anything Elena said.
- Jamie's personal recording was duplicated and sealed at 5:05 PM.
- Jamie was still waiting, alone, at 5:15 PM.
- Jamie's grandmother picked Jamie up at 5:20 PM under standing
  emergency-contact arrangements.
- Elena signed in at the archive at 5:47 PM, asking for Jamie by name.
- Elena recorded a message for Jamie on the full session reel afterward.
- The message states the diversion as a fact, then moves past it — Elena
  doesn't lean on it as an excuse. She states plainly that she didn't do
  what she promised that day, and apologizes for not being there.
- Archive policy has always kept master reels separate from personal
  copies and always allowed families to request them directly; nobody,
  including Jamie, ever had until tonight.

**Plausible interpretations** (reasonable readings the evidence supports
without proving):
- However narrow the gap between 5:20 and 5:47, Elena seems to have meant
  to keep at least part of her promise.
- Elena may have done what she reasonably could once the diversion hit,
  with the rest coming down to timing she didn't fully control.
- Choosing to record a message, once she realized she'd missed Jamie,
  suggests she wanted to be heard eventually — not just to apologize in
  the moment and leave.
- Hearing her acknowledge the anger as fair may be a source of comfort;
  it may also sharpen the question of why so much distance followed.

**Unsupported claims** (the game explicitly flags these as reaching past
the record, without asserting a "correct" alternative):
- That Elena only came because she felt guilty, not because she meant to
  see Jamie — the sign-in log records that she came and when, not why.
- That she could easily have left earlier and simply chose not to — no
  record touches when she left home or what alternatives she had.
- That she knew exactly how to reach Jamie afterward and never tried
  again — nothing in the archive documents the years between that day and
  now.
- That hearing the message now resolves everything and there's nothing
  left to forgive — one afternoon's recording, however honest, doesn't
  settle a relationship's later course.

**Intentionally unresolved** (the surviving materials cannot answer these,
and the game does not secretly know the answer either):
- Why the relationship grew distant in the years after that day.
- Whether that distance connects to this specific afternoon at all, or to
  other things the archive has no record of.
- What the player should feel, or do, now that they've heard the message.

## Closed vs. open questions, concretely

| Question | Type | Where it's answered |
|---|---|---|
| Did Elena come to the archive that day? | Closed | `SR-01` |
| Was she before or after the grandmother's pickup? | Closed | `SR-01` + `RR-04` |
| Did she give a reason for the delay, and is it corroborated? | Closed (partially) | `SR-02` + `SR-03` |
| Could she have left home earlier and avoided it? | Open | Nowhere. No item touches it. |
| Did she leave Jamie anything? | Closed | `RR-03` + `CO-02` + `CO-04` + `LR-01` |
| Had anyone ever asked for the master reel before? | Closed | `LR-02` |
| Why did the relationship grow distant afterward? | Open | Nowhere. |
| How should the player feel about what they've heard? | Open | The game offers interpretations, never a verdict. |

## The final note

The desk scene is not a quiz. Topics 1–3 each offer a "leave this out"
option with no penalty; topic 4 (feelings) is multi-select, because comfort
and regret are not mutually exclusive, and can be left entirely blank. The
generated note is exactly as long as the choices the player made. The one
guardrail is transparency: an option that reaches past the evidence is
labeled and, once chosen, explained (what specifically it invents), rather
than silently accepted as equivalent to a supported fact. The game never
asks the player to decide whether to forgive Elena — only to be precise
about what they actually know, what they're willing to interpret, and what
they're choosing to leave open.

## Auxiliary mechanics and how they serve the design

- **Timeline tab:** every evidence item with a specific clock time attached
  (12 of the 21) automatically lines up in chronological order as it's
  discovered — this is the "timeline reconstruction" mechanic, implemented
  as an automatic sort rather than manual drag-placement, per the brief's
  own note not to require exact positioning.
- **Progressive hints:** each of the three discoveries has three hint
  tiers (point to the question → name the relevant room/evidence → explain
  the connection outright), revealed one at a time on request from the
  Notebook's Discoveries tab, never pushed unprompted.
- **Segment index on the master reel:** the emotionally central recording
  can be jumped to directly ("Session: start" / "Session: end" / "After
  session") rather than requiring a full re-listen, addressing the "don't
  force players through unrelated material" requirement directly.
- **Document flip:** three items (`AR-03`, `PR-01`, `CO-01`) have a second
  side, each adding a small, specific detail rather than restating the
  front — this is the "examine fronts and backs" mechanic.
